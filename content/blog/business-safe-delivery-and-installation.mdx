Picking the safe is the easy part. Getting several hundred pounds of steel into your building, up the steps, through the door and anchored in the right spot is where most people get stuck.

Whether you run a restaurant that needs a drop safe, a retail store handling daily cash, a medical office protecting records, or you simply need a fire-rated safe for the back office, here is what a proper safe delivery and installation looks like, from the team at B&B Locksmith & Security in Walnut Creek.

## Step 1: Choosing the right safe for the job

Businesses usually need one of three kinds:

- **Burglary and fire safes** for documents, cash and valuables. The American Security (AMSEC) CSC1413 we delivered recently is one example, built to resist both break-ins and fire.
- **Drop or depository safes** for restaurants and retail. Staff can drop cash or deposits through a slot without being able to open the safe.
- **Cash-management safes** for higher-volume retail. B&B is Tidel certified, so we can service and support Tidel cash-handling safes.

B&B sells Hollon, AMSEC, Gardall and other brands, so we can match the safe to what you're storing and where it will sit, rather than pushing one model.

## Step 2: Measuring the route, not just the spot

Before delivery day, we look at the full path: doorway widths, steps, turns, thresholds, flooring and the final location. A safe that fits the closet but not the hallway is a common, expensive surprise.

This is also when we check what the floor is made of, because that decides how the safe gets anchored.

## Step 3: Delivery with the right equipment

We also move existing safes: within a building, to a new office, or out when you upgrade.

## Step 4: Leveling and bolting it down

<figure class="my-8"><img src="/images/services/safes/hollon-gun-safe-install.jpg" alt="B&B technician beside an installed Hollon safe with a level on top" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto"/><figcaption style="font-family:var(--f-body);color:#1A1A1A99" class="mt-2 text-sm">Level on top, anchors in the floor.</figcaption></figure>

This is the step that gets skipped most often, and it matters most.

- **Leveling** keeps the door swinging and closing properly, so the bolts line up and the lock isn't strained over time.
- **Bolting down** anchors the safe to the floor so it can't simply be tipped, pried or carried off. A free-standing safe is much easier to steal than most people think.

Many safes come with factory anchor holes for exactly this reason. We anchor into the floor type you have and make sure the safe sits square.

## Step 5: Setup and a quick walkthrough

Once it's in place, we make sure the lock works smoothly and walk you through using it. If you need the combination changed when staff turns over, that's a service we provide too.
