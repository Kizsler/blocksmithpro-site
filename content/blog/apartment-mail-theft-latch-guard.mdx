If mail keeps disappearing from your building, look at the front door before you look at the mailboxes.

On a lot of older apartment buildings in San Francisco, the mailboxes sit inside the entry, behind a locked door. That door is supposed to keep people out. But if there's a gap between the door and the frame, someone can slip a tool in, push the latch back and walk right in. No damage, no broken glass, nothing for the tenants to notice until the mail is gone.

## The Castro building

We got the call from a building in the Castro after mail had been stolen several times. The front entry was a pair of doors with the mailboxes just inside. The weak spot was the latch, which could be reached from the outside.

Changing the lock wouldn't have fixed that. A new lock with the same exposed latch is just as easy to get past.

## What we did

We built a custom latch guard and welded it to the door. We cut and fit the steel on site, so it matched this door and this frame, not a one-size kit. The guard covers the gap where the latch meets the frame, so there's nothing left to pry or slip a tool into.

A latch guard doesn't change how the door works, so tenants use it the same way as before.

## Signs your entry door has the same problem

- You can see the latch or daylight through the gap between the door and the frame
- The door closes but doesn't always latch on its own
- There are pry marks, scratches or bent metal near the lock
- Mail or packages go missing with no sign of a break-in

If any of these sound familiar, the fix may be simpler than a whole new door or a camera system.
