At closing time in a lot of restaurants and shops, the day's cash goes into a bank bag, and the bank bag goes into a desk drawer, a filing cabinet or the manager's car. That's the setup a drop safe is made to replace.

If you run a restaurant, cafe or store in Walnut Creek, Concord, San Ramon or anywhere in the East Bay, here's what a drop safe does and what to look for before you buy one.

## What a drop safe is

A drop safe (also called a depository safe) has two parts: a way to put cash in without opening the safe, and a locked compartment that only certain people can open.

Staff drop envelopes or bags through a slot, a pull-out drawer or a rotating hopper. Once it's in, it falls into the locked body of the safe. The cashier or shift lead can make a drop. Only the manager or owner can open it.

That split is the whole point. Cash leaves the register during the shift, the person closing never has access to the full day's take, and you're not relying on a desk drawer overnight.

## 1. How cash goes in

There are three common designs:

- **Slot.** The simplest. Fine for envelopes, but a slot is the easiest type to fish from if the safe doesn't have a good baffle inside.
- **Drawer.** You pull out a drawer, put the bag in, and push it back. The bag drops in when the drawer closes. Better for thicker bags.
- **Rotary hopper.** A drum that turns to drop the deposit inside. The opening never lines up directly with the inside of the safe, which makes fishing much harder.

Ask to see how the deposit opening is protected. Anti-fishing baffles matter more than the thickness of the door.

## 2. Two compartments or one

Many drop safes have a deposit compartment below and a separate upper compartment for the starting cash drawer, change, or spare keys. If your managers count tills at the start of a shift, the upper compartment saves you a second safe.

## 3. The lock

Most businesses do well with an electronic lock. Look for:

- **Separate user codes,** so each manager has their own and you can delete one when someone leaves.
- **An audit trail,** if you want to see when the safe was opened and by which code.
- **Time delay,** which makes the safe wait several minutes after the code is entered before it opens. It's one of the best things you can do to discourage a robbery, because nobody can open the safe on the spot.

## 4. Smart safes

For higher cash volume, a smart safe takes this further. Bills go into a validator that counts them as they're deposited, so the safe keeps a running total. ## 5. Size and weight

Buy for the cash and bags you'll actually handle, plus room to grow. A safe that's too small leads to bags stuffed into a drawer "just for tonight". Weight matters too, which is why the next point matters most.

## 6. Installation: bolt it down

A drop safe that isn't bolted to the floor can be tipped onto a hand truck and wheeled out the back door. Bolting it down is not optional.

Where it goes matters as well. Put it in a back office or a spot out of view from the counter and the front windows. Plan for the person making the drop to have a clear, private path to the safe.

## FAQ

**What's the difference between a drop safe and a regular safe?** A drop safe lets staff deposit cash without opening the safe. A regular safe has to be opened every time something goes in, which means more people need the code.

**Does a drop safe need to be bolted down?** Yes. A safe that isn't anchored can be tipped and moved. We bolt down every safe we install.

**Can a drop safe go in a small back office?** Usually, yes. We'll measure the space and the path in, and check the floor before recommending a model.

**What is a smart safe?** A smart safe counts bills as they're deposited and keeps a running total. B&B is Tidel certified and can help with Tidel smart safes.
